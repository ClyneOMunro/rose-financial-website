The Survivor Benefit Map, Glossary, and Forms

Every benefit has two questions: is the payout one-time or monthly, and is it taxed. Once you understand how they work, you can understand how you’ll receive your benefits.

Phase · Reference Read time · 8 min Kit · Loss of a Loved One
How to read the maps

Columns split one-time payments from monthly income. Rows split what arrives tax-free from what is taxed. The one-time, tax-free box is money to hold steady while you decide. The monthly boxes are your income plan. The taxed row is where planning can meaningfully change what you actually keep. Amounts marked to confirm depend on your family's specific record.

A note on money raised for you

If friends or your community raise money for your family, that money is generally treated as personal gifts, which means it is not taxed as income, and it does not reduce the benefits above, since none of them are based on your savings. Keep a simple record of the fundraiser and how the money is used, and if any of it came from an employer rather than from individuals, set that portion aside for your accountant to review, because employer-sourced payments can follow different rules.

Glossary: programs and agencies

Short formFull nameWhat it means for you
CAOCasualty Assistance OfficerThe person the military assigns to guide your family and file most claims with you. Your first call.
DoD / DFASDepartment of Defense / Defense Finance and Accounting ServiceDFAS is the pay office: it issues the death gratuity, the Survivor Benefit Plan annuity, and final military pay.
VADepartment of Veterans AffairsPays DIC, education benefits, and burial benefits; also stands behind SGLI.
SGLIServicemembers' Group Life InsuranceMilitary life insurance, up to $500,000, paid tax-free to the named beneficiary.
DICDependency and Indemnity CompensationThe flat-rate, tax-free monthly benefit from the VA. The foundation of a military survivor's income.
SBPSurvivor Benefit PlanThe taxable monthly annuity from DFAS, 55 percent of the retired pay base. Paid in full alongside DIC.
TRICAREMilitary health programCoverage continues: three years at active-duty family rates for a spouse, then retiree rates; children stay at active-duty family status until they age out.
BAHBasic Allowance for HousingThe housing allowance, which continues for 365 days after an active-duty death.
Fry / DEAFry Scholarship / Dependents' Educational AssistanceThe two VA education programs for survivors. A spouse eligible for both chooses one, permanently.
OPMOffice of Personnel ManagementThe federal civilian retirement agency: it pays the FERS death benefit and survivor annuities and manages FEHB continuation.
FERSFederal Employees Retirement SystemThe civilian retirement system. Source of the Basic Employee Death Benefit and the survivor annuity.
BEDBBasic Employee Death BenefitThe FERS lump-sum benefit: half the salary plus a COLA-adjusted flat amount. Taxable, with a payout election to make.
FECAFederal Employees' Compensation ActFederal workers' compensation. When the death was duty-related, its monthly tax-free benefit is often the largest of all.
FEGLIFederal Employees' Group Life InsuranceThe federal civilian life insurance program, administered by MetLife. Claimed on Form FE-6.
FEHBFederal Employees Health BenefitsFederal civilian health coverage. Survivors can often continue it; once dropped, it is hard to get back.
TSPThrift Savings PlanThe federal retirement savings plan, like a 401(k). It passes to the beneficiary on file with the TSP itself.
TAPSTragedy Assistance Program for SurvivorsA national nonprofit offering peer support and guidance to military survivors at no cost. taps.org.

Glossary: Social Security terms

TermWhat it means for you
Survivor benefitMonthly Social Security paid on the deceased's earnings record: to each eligible child, and to you while caring for a child under 16, generally 75 percent of the deceased's full benefit each.
Family maximumA cap on the total paid on one person's record, roughly 150 to 180 percent of the full benefit. When the family is at the cap, adding a benefit for you may not increase the household total, which is why you ask for the numbers both ways.
Earnings limitBefore your full retirement age, your own benefit is reduced if you work: in 2026, $1 withheld per $2 earned above $24,480. The children's benefits are not reduced by your earnings.
Lump-sum death paymentThe one-time $255 payment, claimed within two years of the death.
